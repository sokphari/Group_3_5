    FAQ: Courses for E-Learning Professionals

    An e-learning professional serves in a range of roles that support the creation of structured courses or learning experiences delivered electronically, including online or computer-based learning, virtual classrooms, performance support materials, and digital collaboration and knowledge sharing.

    E-learning professionals support the creation of e-learning training courses and may serve as instructional designers who specialize in creating e-learning curriculum, virtual trainers or facilitators who deliver online training on platforms like Zoom or Microsoft Teams, or learning technologists who develop, assemble, or author e-learning courses using tools like Articulate Storyline or Articulate Rise.

    E-learning professionals often combine learning technology and instructional design skills to create engaging, effective online e-learning courses. Key skill areas include: Instructional design for e-learning training : Applying learning theories and models, needs assessment, writing learning objectives, and structuring and sequencing engaging learner-centric e-learning courses and modules.Instructional design for e-learning training: Applying learning theories and models, needs assessment, writing learning objectives, and structuring and sequencing engaging learner-centric e-learning courses and modules. E-learning authoring and tools : Using authoring tools (for example, Articulate Rise and other e-learning tools) to build interactive, media-rich e-learning courses.E-learning authoring and tools: Using authoring tools (for example, Articulate Rise and other e-learning tools) to build interactive, media-rich e-learning courses.

    E-learning professionals often need project management and collaboration skills to successfully design and build online learning solutions. These skills include planning and managing e-learning training projects, coordinating with stakeholders, and using project management tools and processes to keep work on track.
